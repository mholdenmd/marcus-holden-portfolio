Overview

Play as a Right, Not a Reward

Pangeoria is a SCAD SERVE capstone project built in partnership with Ronald McDonald House Charities Atlanta (Peachtree Dunwoody location), designing three connected experiences for kids staying at the House during a sibling's or their own medical treatment:

  • Pangeoria Explorer — a movement-based AR mobile app with floor-based daily quests
  • Mother Oakeechee — a physical storytelling tree installation with a projected face and audio
  • An immersive projection room

I served as UX Project Co-Lead alongside Chenice Taylor, leading research synthesis, UX strategy, persona work, the quest and reward system design, and process documentation.

Context & Problem

Bored, Isolated, and Full of Energy They Can't Spend

Children staying at RMHC Atlanta face long, uncertain stays in an unfamiliar place. They're bored, cut off from friends, and — per our client contact Alissa Scherb — carrying pent-up physical energy with limited outlets to release it. The design goal was to restore a sense of normal childhood, dignity, and play inside a space built around medical logistics.

Who We Designed For

Leon

The Turning Point

From VR to Movement-Based AR

Pangeoria was originally scoped around VR headsets. We pivoted to movement-based mobile AR — and this pivot is the strongest narrative beat of the case study.

Client Input — Alissa Scherb

Kids at the House have pent-up energy that needs a physical outlet. A headset experience keeps them seated and still; it doesn't solve the actual problem we heard on site.

Team Reasoning — Dignity & Parity

Shared headsets create hygiene, supervision, and access issues. Phones let every kid participate equally, on their own device, without waiting their turn.

Design Philosophy

Three Pillars

01

Movement

Physical activity as the core mechanic — quests exist to get kids up and moving, not sitting and tapping.

02

Stealth Learning

Daily subject-theme rotation embeds learning inside play, so it never feels like schoolwork disguised as a game.

03

Restored Experience

Dignity as a design goal in itself — the point is feeling like a normal kid again, not just being entertained.

The Solution

Quest System, Rewards & the Hide-and-Seek Mechanic

Quests are scoped to the first floor only, with a daily theme rotation rather than a floor-to-floor structure — keeping the experience simple to supervise and easy for staff to reset each day.

Daily Theme Rotation

Themes run Monday through Saturday, each tied to a different subject. Sunday is a free-choice day with no assigned theme.

Scaling Difficulty

Challenges get harder as kids progress, so returning players — often on long stays — keep finding something new to work toward.

Hide-and-Seek Mechanic

Floating AR objects are hidden around the floor and require physical movement to find and scan, turning exploration itself into the gameplay.

Two-Track Rewards

A free flower is always available so no kid leaves empty-handed, alongside diamonds earned through correct answers that can be spent in an in-app Shop on decorative pots for the flower.

Guide Character: Bob the Bobcat

Bob is a pre-existing creature-partner asset from before I joined the project, which I repurposed as an in-app guide — leading kids to the day's quest location and giving the questing a story reason to exist, rather than just a checklist. The original vision was a full roster of creature partners; we scoped down to Bob alone for the demo.

A desktop admin interface was scoped as a future stretch idea for House staff, not a current deliverable.

The Broader Ecosystem

Beyond the App

Pangeoria Explorer is one piece of a larger, team-built experience. I co-led UX for the app; the physical installations below belong to teammates and are credited accordingly.

Mother Oakeechee

A physical storytelling tree installation with a projected face and audio, giving the world a character kids can talk to in person. Built inside the real "Rollins Healing Tree" structure already standing at RMHC Atlanta — Mother Oakeechee is the story name for that same tree.

Led by Cass Bailey

Immersive Projection Room

A projection-based room extending Pangeoria's world beyond the phone screen into the physical space of the House.

Led by Jonatan Galvez

Process

Research & Documentation

We conducted a site visit to RMHC Atlanta, capturing the conversation with Alissa Scherb via Otter.ai as our source of record. The 7-slide UX presentation arc moved through: What We Learned → The Problem → Who We're Designing For → Our Goals → From VR to AR → Our Solution → Prototype. A full process book was maintained in Dropbox Paper, with Marcus owning the UX sections and the roadmap.

Attribution discipline: claims are sourced to what Alissa Scherb told us directly versus what the team reasoned independently, and open questions are labeled as gaps rather than smoothed over.
